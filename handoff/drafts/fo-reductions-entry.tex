\subsection{SAT is NP-complete under first-order reductions, for classes containing the empty structure}
CNF formulas are finite structures with binary relations $\mathrm{pos}(c,x)$ and
$\mathrm{neg}(c,x)$; every element names a clause and a variable, and an element related to
nothing is the empty clause. SAT, the satisfiable structures, has its encoded query language in
NP. Every isomorphism-closed class of finite relational structures whose query language is in NP
\emph{and which contains the empty structure} is first-order reducible to SAT, by grounding its
existential second-order definition in Tseitin style; for such classes, first-order reducibility
to SAT is equivalent to being isomorphism-closed and in NP, and every class first-order reducible
to SAT is in NP. Without the hypothesis, every isomorphism-closed class in NP is first-order
reducible to SAT on the nonempty finite structures. 3SAT (at most three literals per clause) and
3COL (graph structures, loops allowed) are first-order interreducible, and the same interpretation
reduces 3SAT to three-colorability of simple graphs. SAT reduces to independent set on structures
with at least two elements (Karp's occurrence graph); independent set, clique and vertex cover are
existential second-order definable.\citep[Chapter 7]{Immerman1999}\citep{Fagin1974}\citep{GareyJohnson1979}

These are first-order interpretations, with quantifiers, of unordered structures, not first-order
projections of ordered structures as in Immerman; neither statement implies the other. Interpretations
of positive dimension map the empty structure to the empty, satisfiable CNF structure, which is the
reason for the hypothesis. Reductions to 3SAT from SAT, hardness of 3SAT and 3COL, reachability,
and bridges to the encoded problems remain.
