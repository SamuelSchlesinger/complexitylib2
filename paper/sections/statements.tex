\section{Completeness and verification}

\subsection{Cook--Levin and its complement}
SAT is NP-complete and UNSAT is coNP-complete under tidy polynomial-time
many-one reductions, using the library's encoding \lean{encCNF} of CNF formulas.
Reduction efficiency is proved, not assumed.\citep[Section 2.3]{AroraBarak09}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/CookLevin/Completeness.lean}{52}{Cslib.Complexity.CookLevin.SAT_npComplete}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/CookLevin/Completeness.lean}{56}{Cslib.Complexity.CookLevin.UNSAT_coNPComplete}

\subsection{Circuit Value belongs to P}
Given a one-output De Morgan circuit and an input of the matching arity, deciding
whether its output is true is in P. Instances are well formed by their type;
\lean{encCircuitValue} encodes the circuit and input bits. The standard
P-completeness theorem under logspace reductions is not yet formalized here.
\citep{Ladner1975}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Problems/CircuitValue.lean}{105}{Cslib.Complexity.CircuitValue.mem_P}

\subsection{The PCP theorem}
\[
\cc{NP}=\cc{PCP}(O(\log n),O(1)).
\]
The verifier is polynomial-time and nonadaptive, has perfect completeness and
soundness at most $1/2$, and uses logarithmically many random bits and constantly
many queries. In the Lean equality the bound functions are
$\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1$ (with Lean's natural-number logarithm) and $1$;
the class definition allows constant multiples of both. It does not assert
one query.\citep{Dinur2007}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/PCP/Completeness.lean}{81}{Cslib.Complexity.NP_eq_PCP_log}

\section{Space and alternation}

\subsection{Nondeterministic logspace lies in polynomial time}
\[
\cc{NL}\subseteq\cc{P}.
\]
NL is defined by native nondeterministic machines with logarithmic space bounded
on every computation prefix and acceptance by an accepting path. A polynomial-time
decider exists even though the nondeterministic machine's other branches need not
halt. This entry does not assert the still-outstanding native $\cc{NL}=\cc{coNL}$
theorem.\citep[Section 8.5]{Sipser2013}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/LogSpace/PolyTime.lean}{44}{Cslib.Complexity.NL_subset_P}

\subsection{Savitch's theorem for polynomial space}
\[
\cc{NPSPACE}=\cc{PSPACE}.
\]
Both classes are machine-defined. Nondeterministic acceptance is by an accepting
path, with every path respecting the space bound; the deterministic decider is
total. This is the equality of polynomial-space classes, not a statement of the
fully parameterized $\cc{NSPACE}(s)\subseteq\cc{DSPACE}(s^2)$ theorem.
\citep[Corollary 8.6]{Sipser2013}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Space/Savitch.lean}{54}{Cslib.Complexity.NPSPACE_eq_PSPACE}

\subsection{Alternating polynomial time equals polynomial space}
\[
\cc{AP}=\cc{PSPACE}.
\]
AP uses the library's native alternating machines and a polynomial time bound.
The equality is with the same machine-defined PSPACE used above; it is not merely
an equality of abstract game predicates.\citep{ChandraKozenStockmeyer1981}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Alternation/Savitch.lean}{385}{Cslib.Complexity.AP_eq_PSPACE}

\subsection{Interactive proofs lie in polynomial space}
\[
\cc{IP}\subseteq\cc{PSPACE}.
\]
IP uses a private-coin verifier realized by one fair-coin oracle machine and a
polynomial clock valid against every stateful prover. Provers may be randomized
and computationally unbounded; completeness is at least $2/3$ and soundness at
most $1/3$. The converse $\cc{PSPACE}\subseteq\cc{IP}$ is not yet formalized here.
\citep[Chapter 8]{AroraBarak09}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/IP/PSPACE.lean}{620}{Cslib.Complexity.IP_subset_PSPACE}

\section{Randomness, counting and arithmetic circuits}

\subsection{Sipser--G\'acs--Lautemann}
\[
\cc{BPP}\subseteq\Sigma^p_2\cap\Pi^p_2.
\]
For the library's bounded-error polynomial-time class, membership has both an
existential--universal and a universal--existential characterization with
polynomially bounded bit-string witnesses and a polynomial-time predicate.
\citep{Sipser1983,Lautemann1983}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/PH/SipserLautemann.lean}{489}{Cslib.Complexity.BPP_subset_SigmaP_two_inter_PiP_two}

\subsection{Toda's theorem}
\[
\cc{PH}\subseteq\cc{P}^{\#\cc{P}}.
\]
Here $\#\cc{P}$ counts witnesses of polynomial-time verifiers. The target class
uses a polynomial-time oracle program answering decision problems via bits of
one such counting function. The program is fixed before the oracle is chosen
and must work for every oracle language agreeing on valid encoded queries;
off-code answers are arbitrary. The target class has no separate query-count
parameter. The nondeterministic-machine characterization of $\#\cc{P}$
remains separate unfinished work.\citep{Toda1991}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Complexity/Classes/Toda.lean}{121}{Cslib.Complexity.PH_subset_PWithSharpPOracle}

\subsection{Polynomial-size circuits for the determinant}
For every commutative ring $K$ and $n\in\mathbb N$, the generic $n\times n$
determinant has a division-free arithmetic circuit over $K$ of size at most
\[
3n^4+4.
\]
Consequently its polynomial family belongs to $\cc{VP}$ over $K$. The same circuit
evaluates determinants over every commutative $K$-algebra, with matrix entries
supplied in row-major order. This is a gate-count assertion, not a certified
uniform circuit-generation bound.\citep{Bird2011,Valiant1979Algebra}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Determinant.lean}{256}{Cslib.Circuits.Arithmetic.exists_circuit_det}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Determinant.lean}{265}{Cslib.Circuits.Arithmetic.exists_circuit_det_eval}
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Arithmetic/Determinant.lean}{281}{Cslib.Circuits.Arithmetic.detFamily_mem_VP}

\section{Circuits for sparse counter modifications}

Write $W_n=\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z$ for the $n$-bit words, represented
least-significant bit first, and let $c(x)=x+1$. For maps on $W_n$, put
\[
D(f,g)=\{x\in W_n:f(x)\ne g(x)\}.
\]
Circuit size below counts native De Morgan gates, including constants,
comparisons, selection and addition. These are nonuniform existence results:
they provide no efficient algorithm for constructing the correction table.
The port follows Schlesinger's iterated-functions development; the source credits Moreno and Rivera for the related block characterization
of almost-commuting permutations.
\citep{Schlesinger2026IteratedFunctions,MorenoRivera2016}

\subsection{Maps commuting with an edited counter}
For any maps $f,h:W_n\to W_n$, if $h$ is injective and $h\circ f=f\circ h$, then
there is a circuit computing all $n$ output bits of $h$ with size at most
\[
100(n+1)(|D(f,c)|+1).
\]
There is no assumption that $f$ is a permutation or that a small circuit for $f$
is supplied. The assertion includes $n=0$.
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/Iteration/SparseDisplacement.lean}{197}{Cslib.Circuits.Boolean.Iteration.sparse_counter_centralizer}

\subsection{Every fixed signed power}
For every permutation $f$ of $W_n$ and every integer $T$, a circuit computes $f^T$
with size at most
\[
100(n+1)(|D(f,c)|+1).
\]
The size bound is independent of the magnitude and sign of $T$. The quantifiers
are ``for every $T$, there exists a circuit'': $T$ is fixed, not an additional
input to a single circuit.
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/Iteration/SparseDisplacement.lean}{208}{Cslib.Circuits.Boolean.Iteration.sparse_counter_power}

\subsection{Transfer through supplied coordinates}
Let $f,g,e$ be permutations of $W_n$ such that $e(g(x))=e(x)+1$ for every $x$.
Suppose circuits $E$ and $E^{-1}$ compute $e$ and its inverse, respectively.
For every $T\in\mathbb Z$, there is a circuit for $f^T$ with size at most
\[
|E|+|E^{-1}|+100(n+1)(|D(f,g)|+1).
\]
Both coordinate conversions are charged. The theorem does not assert that an
arbitrary cycle has cheap coordinates or complete the general iterated-function
circuit-bound program.
\decl{Complexity/Computability/Circuit/Boolean/Iteration/SparseDisplacement.lean}{217}{Cslib.Circuits.Boolean.Iteration.sparse_coordinate_power}
