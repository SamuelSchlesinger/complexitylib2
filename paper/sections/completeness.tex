\begin{result}
\subsection{Parsimonious Cook--Levin and complete counting problems}
The Cook--Levin tableau is parsimonious: for a deterministic multi-tape machine $M$, input
prefix $u$, certificate length $L$ and step bound $T$, the tableau has exactly as many
satisfying assignments (of the variables occurring in it) as there are words
$w\in\{0,1\}^L$ such that $M$ on $uw$ has halted with output $1$ after $T$ steps. Hence the
existing Cook--Levin reduction of a verifier $V$ maps an instance $a$ to a CNF formula with
exactly $|\{w\in\{0,1\}^{p(|a|)} : V(a,w)=1\}|$ satisfying assignments, and $\#\mathrm{SAT}$
is $\#\cc{P}$-complete under parsimonious polynomial-time reductions $f(a)=\#\mathrm{SAT}(r(a))$,
for the verifier-based $\#\cc{P}$ (certificates of exact polynomial length) and every
injective instance encoding. The count ranges over assignments of the occurring variables.
Consequently, under polynomial-time many-one reductions: formulas with an odd number of
satisfying assignments form a $\oplus\cc{P}$-complete problem; unique satisfiability is
$\cc{US}$-complete; a count not divisible by $k$ is $\cc{Mod}_k\cc{P}$-complete and its
complement $\cc{coMod}_k\cc{P}$-complete, for every modulus $k$ (including the degenerate
moduli $0$ and $1$ of the total definition); pairs $(\varphi,m)$, $m$ in binary, with
$\#\varphi\ge m$, as well as $\mathrm{MAJSAT}$ (CNF formulas $\varphi$ satisfied by strictly
more than half of the $2^{|\mathrm{vars}(\varphi)|}$ assignments to their occurring variables,
$2^{|\mathrm{vars}(\varphi)|} < 2\cdot\#\varphi$, via an odd/even variable-relabelling and
selector-clause padding construction), form $\cc{PP}$-complete problems; and pairs with
$\#\varphi=m$ form a
$\cc{C_{=}P}$-complete problem whose complement is $\cc{coC_{=}P}$-complete. All classes are the
library's verifier-based definitions, and variable counts range over the variables occurring in
$\varphi$.

\begin{result}
\subsection{DP and SAT--UNSAT completeness}
The difference class $\cc{DP}$ consists of languages $B\cap C$ with
$B\in\cc{NP}$ and $C\in\cc{coNP}$, equivalently differences of two NP
languages. For any NP-complete $B$ and coNP-complete $C$, the product language
$B\times C$ is DP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, using
the product encoding. In particular, pairs of CNF formulas $(\varphi,\psi)$
with $\varphi$ satisfiable and $\psi$ unsatisfiable form a DP-complete problem.
The theorem quantifies over all injectively encoded source types; the two
component reductions and their pairing have certified polynomial time.
The definition permits distinct NP and coNP components; it does not require
the resulting language to belong to both classes.\citep{Papadimitriou94}

\begin{result}
\subsection{The Boolean hierarchy over NP}
For an injectively encoded universe $X$, using the difference-hierarchy
convention and complements relative to $X$,
\[
\cc{BH}_0=\{\varnothing\},\qquad
\cc{BH}_{n+1}=\{B\setminus D:B\in\cc{NP},\ D\in\cc{BH}_n\},\qquad
\cc{coBH}_n=\{A\subseteq X:X\setminus A\in\cc{BH}_n\}.
\]
We prove $\cc{coBH}_0=\{X\}$, $\cc{BH}_1=\cc{NP}$, $\cc{coBH}_1=\cc{coNP}$,
$\cc{BH}_2=\cc{DP}$, and $\cc{coBH}_2=\cc{coDP}$; monotonicity of
$\cc{BH}_n$ and $\cc{coBH}_n$; the interleaving containments
$\cc{BH}_n\subseteq\cc{coBH}_{n+1}$ and $\cc{coBH}_n\subseteq\cc{BH}_{n+1}$
(hence $\bigcup_n\cc{coBH}_n=\bigcup_n\cc{BH}_n=\cc{BH}$, which is closed under
complement); right-hand closure with $\cc{NP}$ languages ($A\cap B\in\cc{BH}_n$
and $A\setminus B,A\cup B\in\cc{BH}_{n+1}$ for $A\in\cc{BH}_n$ and
$B\in\cc{NP}$); and polynomial-time many-one reduction closure for $\cc{BH}_n$,
$\cc{coBH}_n$, and $\cc{BH}$. We also prove the level-collapse equivalences:
\[
\cc{BH}_{k+1}\subseteq\cc{BH}_k
\iff \cc{coBH}_k\subseteq\cc{BH}_k
\iff \cc{BH}_k=\cc{coBH}_k
\iff \cc{BH}_{k+1}=\cc{BH}_k,
\]
and when $\cc{coBH}_k\subseteq\cc{BH}_k$, every higher level collapses
($\cc{BH}_m=\cc{coBH}_m=\cc{BH}_k$ for all $m\ge k$) and the full hierarchy
equals $\cc{BH}_k$ (in particular, $\cc{NP}=\cc{coNP}\implies\cc{BH}=\cc{NP}$).
On an inhabited universe $X$ the $k=0$ collapse premises are false since
$X\ne\varnothing$, whereas on an empty universe every level is $\{\varnothing\}$.
The difference-hierarchy construction, inverse-image closure, right-hand
intersection/difference/union closure, and upward level collapse are proved
generically over any base class of sets satisfying the corresponding closure
hypotheses, and instantiated at $\cc{NP}$. Strictness of the levels and Kadin's
collapse of $\cc{PH}$ from a Boolean-hierarchy collapse are not
formalized.\citep{CaiEtAl1988,Kadin1988}

\subsection{Exponential lower bounds for graph problems under ETH}
Assume the exponential time hypothesis. Then for each of the following problems there is
$\delta>0$ such that it is not decidable within time $2^{\delta\mu}$ times a polynomial in the
length of the encoded instance: three-colorability, independent set and vertex cover (graph with
binary target), and directed and undirected Hamiltonian cycle and path (graph with endpoints for
paths), all with $\mu=|V|+|E|$; independent set and clique with $\mu=|V|$. A graph is a list of
declared vertices and a list of edges, and $|V|$, $|E|$ are the list lengths with repetitions;
they bound the distinct counts, so the bounds for distinct counts follow. The predicate negated
allows the decider to depend on $\delta$. Each bound composes a polynomial-time reduction from
3-CNF formulas whose output measure is linear in the number $m$ of clauses with the
sparsification lemma, under which ETH excludes $2^{o(m)}$ for 3SAT. Three-colorability uses the
gadget reduction (at most $(24k+12)m+9$ vertices and edges for clauses of width at most $k$);
independent set, vertex cover and clique use the variable-edge reduction of Garey and Johnson
(at most $(k^2+5k)m$), not Karp's occurrence graph, which can have quadratically many edges.
Hamiltonian problems use a variant of Sipser's diamond construction with one diamond per
distinct variable and one slot per occurrence; its directed path graph has at most $(23k+1)m+1$
vertices and arcs in total, and the cycle and undirected graphs obtained by closing and splitting
it stay linear (at most $(115k+5)m+10$ for undirected Hamiltonian cycle). The correctness of
diamond graphs is proved once for arbitrary row layouts, and Sipser's construction, with
$\Theta(m^2)$ vertices in the worst case, is an instance of it. Garey and Johnson's reduction from
vertex cover to Hamiltonian cycle is not used: its selector edges are $\Theta(m^2)$ on these
instances.

\begin{result}
\subsection{Fixed-parameter tractability}
A parameterized problem is a problem with an encoding of its instances and a parameter
$\kappa$ from instances to $\mathbb{N}$. Write $k=\kappa(a)$ and $L$ for the length of the encoded
instance. The problem is in $\cc{FPT}$ when a multi-tape machine over $\{0,1\}$ decides it within
time $O(f(k)\,(L+1)^c)$, and in $\cc{XP}$ within time $O(f(k)\,(L+1)^{g(k)})$, for a constant $c$
and functions $f,g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$. The functions are arbitrary, as in Downey and
Fellows' uniform tractability; the variant requiring computable $f$ (Flum--Grohe, Cygan et al.)
is not formalized. For every parameter, $\cc{P}\subseteq\cc{FPT}\subseteq\cc{XP}$, and both classes
equal $\cc{P}$ when the parameter is bounded by a constant. $\cc{FPT}$ is closed under complement,
intersection and union, and $\cc{XP}$ under complement. If $\kappa\le h\circ\kappa'$ for a
function $h$, tractability in $\kappa$ gives tractability in $\kappa'$, and $f$ may be taken
monotone. A decider within time $2^{\delta k}$ times a polynomial in $L$, for any real $\delta$, is
an fpt decider, so SAT and $k$-SAT are in $\cc{FPT}$ parameterized by the number of distinct
variables. An fpt-reduction is a membership-preserving map, tidily computable within time
$O(F(k)\,(L+1)^c)$, whose output parameter is at most $h(k)$, with $F$ and $h$ arbitrary rather
than computable; polynomial-time reductions with such a parameter bound are fpt-reductions.
Fpt-reducibility is a preorder, and $\cc{FPT}$ and $\cc{XP}$ are closed under it. The $\cc{W}$
hierarchy is not covered.

\subsection{Kernelization and parameterized vertex cover}
A kernelization of a parameterized problem $(A,\kappa)$ is a membership-preserving map $K$, tidily
computable in polynomial time, whose output has both encoded length and parameter at most $g(k)$
for an arbitrary size bound $g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ (or a polynomially bounded $g$ for a
polynomial kernel), where $k=\kappa(a)$ and $L=|\mathrm{enc}(a)|$; every kernelization is an
fpt-reduction from the problem to itself. Over a nonempty type of instances, every problem in
$\cc{FPT}$ has a kernel (running the fpt decider for $(c+1)(L+1)^{e+1}$ steps on the universal
multi-tape machine outputs a fixed yes or no instance on halting and leaves the instance unchanged
on timeout, when $L<f(k)$), and a problem decidable in $\cc{DTIME}(\mathrm{enc},t)$ for some
$t\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ is in $\cc{FPT}$ if and only if it has a kernel
(Cai--Chen--Downey--Fellows); problems in $\cc{P}$ have constant-size polynomial kernels. For
Vertex Cover (encoded graphs with binary target $k$, requiring a cover of size $k$ when $k$ is at
most the number $n$ of distinct declared vertices), a bounded search tree of depth $\min(n,k)$
branches on the endpoints of the first uncovered edge over $2^{\min(n,k)}$ root-to-leaf paths and
decides the problem within time $O(2^k\,(L+1)^e)$, placing Vertex Cover in $\cc{FPT}$
parameterized by $k$. The computable-$g$ variant and explicit polynomial kernels for Vertex Cover
(such as Buss's $O(k^2)$ kernel) are not covered.

\begin{result}
\textbf{Circuit evaluation is P-complete.}
Given a one-output De Morgan circuit with $n$ inputs and an input in
$\{0,1\}^n$, encoded by \lean{encCircuitValue}, deciding whether the output is
true is in P. Every problem in P, under any encoding of its instances, reduces to
it by a logarithmic-space many-one reduction, so it is P-complete under logspace
reductions, and it lies in L exactly when $\cc{P}=\cc{L}$ for every encoding.
Correctness is required on encoded instances. The machine model has read-only input,
write-only output and deterministic multi-tape computation over $\{0,1\}$.
Logspace charges work-tape cells only, with bound
$c(\lfloor\log_2\max\{m,1\}\rfloor+1)$ for encoded input length $m$.
The reduction maps an instance $a$ with $m=|\mathrm{enc}(a)|$ to the tableau
circuit of a polynomial-time decider that halts within $C(m+1)^d$ steps,
run for $T=C(m+1)^d+m+1$ steps, with $\mathrm{enc}(a)$ as its input bits
rather than hardwired constants. The circuit outputs whether the machine
has written exactly $[\mathsf{true}]$ after $T$ steps. The construction is uniform:
each gate is computed from its number within work space linear in the binary
lengths of $m$, $T$ and the number, and a read machine writes the gates in turn. It
works directly on multi-tape configurations, with no conversion to single-tape or
oblivious machines. Monotone Circuit Value is addressed in the next entry. The NC
consequences and first-order-reduction formulation remain outstanding.
\citep{Ladner1975,AroraBarak09,Papadimitriou94}

\textbf{Uniform compilation of polynomial-time function families.}
Let $\mathrm{enc}:\alpha\hookrightarrow\{0,1\}^*$ be any injective instance encoding.
Suppose $n,m:\alpha\to\mathbb N$ are polynomial-time computable in unary, and
$F:\alpha\times\{0,1\}^*\to\{0,1\}^*$ is polynomial-time computable under
\texttt{encPair enc encBits} and \lean{encBits}, using the tidy deterministic
multi-tape machine model. There is a family of De Morgan circuits $C_a$ with
$n(a)$ inputs and $m(a)$ designated outputs whose codes are polynomial-time
computable from $\mathrm{enc}(a)$, such that on every input $x\in\{0,1\}^{n(a)}$,
output $j<m(a)$ is the $j$th bit of $F(a,x)$, or false if that bit is absent.
In particular, if $|F(a,x)|=m(a)$ on the input cube, the whole output is exactly
$F(a,x)$. No decoder or efficient recognition test for the instance encoding's
image is assumed.

The compiler reuses the uniform tableau writer and existing circuit composition.
Its raw-code adapters hardwire an input prefix with exactly two shared constant
gates, combine circuits on shared inputs, and bundle equal-sized blocks.
Their construction times are certified with binary wire numbers and layout
parameters. The resulting family construction is polynomial-time; no depth bound,
optimized gate bound, or runtime claim about Lean's compiled implementation is made.
The machine simulation follows Ladner's construction and the standard
polynomial-time-to-circuit argument; machine certification builds on
Reitwiessner's tidy calculus.\citep{Ladner1975,AroraBarak09,Papadimitriou94}

\subsection{Monotone Circuit Value}

\subsection{The PCP theorem}

\cc{NP}=\cc{PCP}(O(\log n),O(1)).

\subsection{A constant gap for MAX-3SAT}
